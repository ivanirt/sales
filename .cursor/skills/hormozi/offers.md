# Grand slam offer

An offer is what they get, how fast, how certain, and what they risk. A grand slam offer is priced high and still feels like a steal because the result is big, believable, fast, and easy.

## Value equation

```
Value = (Dream outcome × Perceived likelihood) / (Time delay × Effort and sacrifice)
```

Raise the top. Crush the bottom. Do this to the offer, not with adjectives.

- Dream outcome: their words, one result, a finish line they can picture.
- Likelihood: proof, a mechanism, a named process, a guarantee. Specific beats hype.
- Time delay: a first win inside days, not "over the journey."
- Effort and sacrifice: you do the hard part, or you remove steps, decisions, and embarrassment.

If price is the objection and the equation is weak, the offer is the problem.

## Build sequence

1. Write the dream outcome in one line.
2. List every obstacle between them and that outcome. Include fear, time, skill, spouse, cash flow, and "I already tried."
3. Attach one solution to each obstacle.
4. For each solution, pick a delivery vehicle you can fulfill: done-for-you, done-with-you, a tool, a script, a template, access, or a checklist. Score it on the value equation. Prefer faster and easier if margin still works.
5. Trim. Cut anything that does not move the equation or that explodes cost, time, or quality risk. First version should over-deliver on a narrow result.
6. Stack what remains into one offer. They should think "I get all this for only that?"
7. Price from value and delivery cost. A large gap between cost to fulfill and price is the point, as long as the buyer still wins big.
8. Add enhancers last.

## Enhancers

Use these after the stack is real.

- **Scarcity.** A true cap: seats, spots, inventory, your calendar. Say the number. When it is gone, it is gone.
- **Urgency.** A real deadline: cohort start, price change on a date, seasonal window. Rolling fake timers train buyers to wait.
- **Bonuses.** Each bonus kills one specific reason they would say no. Name the bonus by the problem it removes, not by "module 4."
- **Guarantee.** Make the risk reversal stronger than the price fear, and only promise what you will actually pay.
  - Unconditional: refund inside a window. Use when fulfillment cost is low or refunds are rare and you can stomach them.
  - Conditional: they must do the work (show up, submit the inputs, hit the actions). Best default for services.
  - Anti-guarantee: all sales final, said clearly, when the asset is consumed immediately or custom work starts at once.
  - Performance: you keep working, or you pay, until the named result. Only if you control the variables.
- **Name.** Say who it is for, the result, the time frame, and the container (challenge, sprint, install, audit). Skip clever. A stranger should know what they get.

## Commodity test

If a competitor can swap their logo onto your promise and nothing breaks, you are selling the commodity. Change the vehicle, the speed, the risk, or the specificity of the avatar. Do not "explain the value" harder.

## Pricing defaults

- Charge what the result is worth to a buyer who gets it. Compare to the cost of the problem staying unsolved.
- Raise price before you add more delivery, when close rate is high and fulfillment is straining.
- If people say yes too fast, the price is low or the avatar is too easy. Test a higher price on the next ten conversations.
- If people love it and still do not buy, fix likelihood and risk before you cut price.
