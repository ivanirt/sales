# Money model

A money model is a sequence of offers. One offer gets customers. A sequence gets the cash to buy the next customers and the profit to keep.

Run them in this order. Skip a stage only when the prior stage already prints cash.

1. **Attraction** turns a stranger into a customer.
2. **Upsell** gets more cash from someone who already said yes.
3. **Downsell** turns a no into a yes without pretending the premium never existed.
4. **Continuity** keeps them paying so lifetime gross profit compounds.

Present the next offer when they are hottest: right after the yes, the win, or the moment of pain. Do not save the upsell for a vague "later."

## Attraction — pick one

- **Win your money back.** They pay. If they hit a defined result inside a defined window and follow the rules, they can get the fee back or credit it. You get cash, a case study, and a reason for them to do the work. Credit the win into the next purchase when you can.
- **Giveaway.** A real prize for entering. The prize is the tripwire. The application or the event is where you sell the core.
- **Decoy.** A cheap or free stripped version next to the premium. The decoy makes the premium the obvious buy for anyone who wants the result. The decoy must be a real product, not a fake door.
- **Buy X get Y free.** Advertise the free quantity. Price the bundle so the economics work. People anchor on "free," then buy the bundle.
- **Pay less now or pay more later.** A lower price if they pay today, or a higher price if they wait and decide after. The later price is the real price. The early price is the incentive to decide.
- **Free goodwill.** Give a complete useful thing first when trust is the bottleneck. The next offer sells the implementation.

## Upsell — pick one

Offer it immediately after the first yes.

- **Classic.** "You want X. People who get the best results also get Y." Y solves the next problem the first purchase creates.
- **Menu.** Unsell what they do not need, prescribe what they do. Ask what they have already tried. Then recommend one configuration.
- **Anchor.** Show the expensive complete version first. The core offer feels small next to it. The anchor must be something you will actually sell.
- **Rollover.** Apply what they already paid (challenge fee, deposit, prior credit) to a longer or larger purchase so the "win" does not walk out the door.

## Downsell — after a real no

Ask what is in the way. Then:

- **Payment plan.** Same offer, split. "How much can you put down today?" Get a card and a schedule. More payments, more drop-off, so keep the count small and the first payment meaningful.
- **Trial with a penalty.** Free or cheap if they complete the setup actions. If they skip the actions, the fee hits. Gets the right customers to invest effort.
- **Feature downsell.** Lower price by removing a feature (guarantee, speed, done-for-you, seats). Say what they lose. Often they climb back to the full offer once the missing piece is visible. Never silently discount the same thing.

## Continuity — keep them

Sell continuity at the peak of the first result, not after they have gone cold.

- **Bonus.** Join the ongoing plan and get the bonus (or the first program) as the incentive. The bonus is immediate. The plan is the business.
- **Discount for term.** A lower rate or free time for committing to a term today. Month-to-month stays at the full rate.
- **Waived fee.** Month-to-month has a large setup fee. A one-year commit waives it. They initial that leaving early makes the waived fee due. The fee must be real on the month-to-month option.

## Stacking rules

Add a revenue stream only when it uses demand you already created and adds little delivery load: an affiliate handoff, a template, a group call, a tool. If the new offer needs a new team, a new promise, and a new funnel, it is a second business. Say no until the core model hits CFA level 2 or 3 in [economics.md](economics.md).

Map every offer to one job: get the customer, get more cash now, save the no, or get paid again. If it does none of those, cut it.
