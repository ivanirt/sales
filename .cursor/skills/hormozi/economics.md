# Unit economics

You grow by getting more customers, making each one worth more, and getting the cash back faster. The third one is what lets a small business scale without a rich uncle.

## Definitions

**Gross profit (one sale)** = price collected − cost to deliver that sale. Delivery cost is labor, product, shipping, and contractor cost for that customer. It is not rent, software, or your salary.

**Lifetime gross profit (LTGP)** = gross profit you collect from a customer over the whole relationship. For a subscription, use average months retained × monthly gross profit. If you do not know retention, use the worst honest number, not the hope.

**CAC** = everything you spend to get one new customer. Include ads, promoters, creative, commissions, sales salaries allocated to acquisition, and the tools those people need. "Organic" still has a cost if a human made it. Split CAC by channel.

**Payback period** = how long until gross profit from that customer equals CAC.

**30-day gross profit** = gross profit you actually collect in the first 30 days, not contracted revenue that shows up in month six.

## Customer-financed acquisition

Compare 30-day gross profit to CAC.

| Level | 30-day gross profit vs CAC | What it means | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Less than CAC | You front the money. Growth eats cash. | Fix the offer and the money model before you scale. |
| 2 | About equal to CAC | A credit card can float it. The limit is your ad budget. | Push upsells and 30-day continuity until you clear level 3. |
| 3 | About 2× CAC or more | This customer pays for the next ones. | Spend. The new constraint is fulfillment. |

Level 3 is the target before "scale the ads." Doubling customers every month only works if you reinvest and can deliver.

## Levers, in the order that usually pays

1. Raise 30-day gross profit (price, upsell, downsell, faster collection).
2. Raise LTGP (continuity, churn, second purchase) once 30-day cash is not embarrassing.
3. Lower CAC (better offer conversion, kill expensive channels, better avatar).
4. Only then, spend more.

A business with a 10:1 LTGP:CAC that takes 11 months to pay back will still die if the founder is broke. Speed first when cash is tight. Multiple first when cash is already coming back inside 30 days.

## Avatar cut

If they have customers already:

- Rank buyers by gross profit and how long they stay.
- Describe the top slice in concrete terms: situation, trigger, where they came from, what they bought first.
- Stop feeding channels that only produce the bottom slice.
- Rewrite the offer so a top-slice buyer recognizes themselves in the first sentence.
